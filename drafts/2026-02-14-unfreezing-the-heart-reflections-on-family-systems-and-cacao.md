I’m sitting here with a warm cup of cacao, the steam curling up as I process the weight of the past few days. Returning to loved ones after time away—it’s like stepping into a river you thought you knew, only to find the current’s shifted. The stories, the reunions, the quiet moments of clarity—they’ve all stirred something deep. And then there’s this oracle casting for the day, with “Decrease” and “The Taming Power of the Small,” whispering about sacrifice and subtle influence. It feels oddly fitting. Let me unpack this a bit.

### The Weight of Family Stories
A few days ago, I sat with my mom as she recounted the hardships of our childhood—the kind of struggles that shaped us, even if I didn’t fully grasp them at the time. She spoke of my aunts, who passed last year, and the relentless grind my parents endured, sleeping just four hours a night for years while scaling their business. I could feel the heaviness of it all settle into my chest. And for one of those rare moments—maybe the first since middle school—I teared up in front of her. I’d made a pact with myself back then to never cry, to fortify those walls. But something’s shifted recently. I think the time spent in solitude, sitting with sensations and practicing equanimity, has unfrozen parts of me. The vagal nerves, once so tightly wound, are loosening—allowing me to just feel what arises, without the old defenses kicking in.

Key observation: Sometimes, strength isn’t in holding it together. It’s in letting the walls crumble and experiencing what’s on the other side. Have you ever had a moment where you let go of a shield you didn’t even know you were carrying?

### Ancient Frameworks and Modern Systems
Then there was the visit to Peter at his shop. This guy—he’s been a mentor since 2020, just before the world turned upside down with Covid. We dove into the heavenly stems and earthly branches, an ancient framework he introduced me to years ago. I shared how I’ve been applying it, and he peeled back another layer, this time tying it to the positions of the stars. Mind-bending stuff, but what stuck with me more was his management philosophy. He’s got friends in high places—board members, CXOs—and he passed on a gem: integrity in a system matters. If you’re lenient with someone not pulling their weight, you’re actually being cruel to everyone else holding it together. That hit hard, especially as I think about the cacao project I’m working on. He suggested finding specialists to translate cacao into niche industries, to boost sales volume. It’s a nudge to build a tighter, more intentional system around this.

Here’s what that looks like in practice: Whether it’s a business or a personal journey, every participant needs to carry their share—or the whole structure wobbles. How do you balance compassion with accountability in your own systems?

### Governance, Excellence, and Cacao Innovation
Reuniting with my old coach and teammates from the national basketball team brought another angle. One of them mentioned how PSA alumni—folks from the Port Authority of Singapore—are running ports worldwide. Singapore’s governance model and work ethic are seen as gold standards, replicated globally. It got me thinking about the cacao project. What if one of the innovations isn’t just the product, but applying a Singapore-style governance model to a supply chain stretching from the Amazon rainforest to the U.S.? Imagine that level of excellence and consistency threaded through every step. It’s ambitious, but it feels like a clear direction—one the universe keeps pointing me toward.

### Cycles of Grief and Healing
Then there were the personal reunions. Catching up with Ricky, a childhood friend, I saw him stuck in another of his cycles, grappling with grief in the romantic arena. I shared how a steady ritual of drinking cacao over time has been therapeutic for me—how it seems to tap into the vagal nerve system, helping surface and process emotions like grief. It’s not a fix, but a gentle opener. I don’t know if he’ll try it, but sometimes just planting a seed is enough.

And then Garis, another old friend, had a practical suggestion for the cacao project: serve drinks directly in the U.S. market instead of just selling beans. I admitted to him how my lack of drive lately ties into the sadness around my situation with my daughter. He pointed out something stark—how the window to pivot back into software might’ve closed with AI’s rapid rise. I’d hesitated on full-time Silicon Valley gigs before, not wanting to shut the door on being physically present for my daughter. But with the current setup, the logistics, the cash reserves, and the single sales channel for cacao in the U.S., there’s really only one path forward. It’s like the oracle’s “Decrease”—a call to sacrifice, to focus on what’s essential. Two small bowls for the offering. Narrowing down to the core.

Reflections for the day: Sometimes the universe doesn’t just suggest a path—it clears away all the others until you can’t miss it. What’s the one path you’re seeing more clearly right now?

### Frogs in Boiling Pots
Garis, though—he’s caught in his own cycle. Driving for Uber, he’s like a frog in a slowly boiling pot. As unemployment rises, more people turn to gig work, shrinking his margins. He’s got two months before his car’s COE expires, forcing him to stop. It’s a stark reminder of how systems can trap us if we don’t adapt. I’m sitting with that as I think about my own next steps—how to avoid boiling slowly in my own pot.

### Taming the Small and Moving Forward
Looking at the oracle again—“The Taming Power of the Small” speaks of subtle influence, of dense clouds but no rain yet. It feels like a metaphor for where I’m at. There’s potential, a gathering energy, but the downpour hasn’t come. Maybe it’s about small, intentional moves—two bowls for sacrifice, not a grand feast. Small steps with the cacao project, small openings in my emotional walls, small shifts in how I see the systems around me.

I’m left wondering: What small taming power are you wielding right now? What subtle shift could change the current for you?
