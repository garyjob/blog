The cards—or rather, the hexagrams—landed on 52, *Keeping Still*, and 20, *Contemplation*. The words hit me like a pause button: "Keeping his back still" and "The ablution has been made, but not yet the offering." There’s something grounding in that, isn’t there? If you’re curious, you can see the full reading or cast your own here: [https://oracle.truesight.me/?reading=8-8-9-8-6-7](https://oracle.truesight.me/?reading=8-8-9-8-6-7).

**Title: The Art of Doing Nothing – Reflections on Stillness and Contemplation**

I’ve been mulling over this draw all morning. *Keeping Still* feels like a direct nudge to step back from the hustle, the endless grind of chasing Big Hairy Audacious Goals—or BHAGs, as the startup world loves to call them. I caught myself recently, almost instinctively framing every idea, every project, through that aggressive, go-getter lens. Why? It’s not like I woke up one day and decided to be a Silicon Valley cliché. But here I am, realizing how much of an echo chamber I’ve wandered into. My social feeds are curated chaos—full of hustle porn, productivity hacks, and people shouting about “10x-ing” everything. I bet yours don’t look quite the same, do they?

**Key Observation: The Trap of Striving**
The judgment of *Keeping Still* is pretty blunt—don’t waste energy on unnecessary movement. I’m starting to see how much of my recent striving has been just that: wasted motion. I’ve been pushing to define the path forward for a project that, honestly, feels more organic than strategic. It’s like trying to map a river before it’s even decided where to flow. Reading this hexagram, I’m reminded that sometimes the natural path reveals itself only when you stop trying to carve it out with a bulldozer.

**Reflections on Contemplation**
Then there’s *Contemplation*—the idea of preparing but not yet acting, of standing in trust and looking up for guidance. It’s a quieter energy, almost reverent. I picture myself years ago, sitting on a hill in Penang during a rare quiet evening, just watching the city lights flicker below. No agenda, no to-do list. Just being there. That’s the vibe this hexagram pulls out of me. It’s a call to observe, to let the ablution—the cleansing—happen before rushing to the offering. In tech terms, it’s like debugging your code by stepping away from the keyboard for a bit. Sometimes clarity comes in the stillness, not the typing.

**Echo Chambers and Breaking Free**
Here’s where it gets personal—I’m wondering how much of my default “push forward” mindset is just a reflection of the noise I’ve surrounded myself with. The BHAG framework, the growth-at-all-costs mentality—it’s seductive, sure, but it’s also a trap when applied to something that needs to breathe and evolve on its own. I’m guessing my Twitter feed, my LinkedIn scroll, even the podcasts I queue up, are reinforcing this bias. It’s a humbling thought: how much of what I think is “my idea” is just the algorithm talking? What about you—are you noticing the same kind of echo in your own spaces?

**Wrapping Up: A Commitment to Pause**
This reading has me rethinking my approach. I’m going to try leaning into *Keeping Still* for a while—less forcing, more watching. I’ll let this project, this idea, this whatever-it-is, show me where it wants to go. And I’ll take *Contemplation* as a reminder to trust the process, to prepare without overacting. It’s not easy, especially when every other post I see is screaming “move faster!” But maybe the real move is to not move at all, at least for now.

What’s your take—do you ever feel caught in an echo chamber of your own? How do you step back and find stillness when the world’s yelling at you to keep pushing? I’d love to hear.
