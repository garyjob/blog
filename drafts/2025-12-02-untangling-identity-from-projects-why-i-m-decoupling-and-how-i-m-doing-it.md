### Title: "Untangling Identity from Projects: Why I'm Decoupling (and How I'm Doing It)"

#### Intro: When You and Your Project Become One
I remember the early days of my current venture'sitting at my desk late at night, brainstorming ideas, messaging close friends in my network for feedback, and just *being* the project. If someone thought of the project, they thought of me. And honestly' That felt great at first. It was personal, it was raw, and it gave me a sense of ownership that fueled those long hours. But here's the thing I've noticed'when your identity gets so wrapped up in a project, it's hard for either of you to grow independently. Key observation: A project needs space to scale beyond the founder, and the founder needs space to be more than just the project.

Now that my venture has grown to a somewhat sizable level, I've realized it's time to be deliberate about decoupling my identity from it. #### Section 1: Why Decoupling Matters
Here's the deal'when a project is small, having your identity tied to it can be a superpower. It's your baby, your vision, and that personal connection drives you to hustle harder. People associate the work with you, and that builds trust fast. But as the project scales, that tight coupling can become a bottleneck. The project can't take on new voices or ideas if it's always seen as 'Gary's thing.' And on the flip side, I can't explore new passions or ideas if I'm always tethered to this one identity.

Reflections for the day: Scaling isn't just about numbers or revenue'it's about letting go of control in the right ways. It's about trusting that the project can stand on its own, and that I can be more than its face. If I don't decouple now, I risk stunting its growth'and my own. #### Section 2: My First Steps to Decouple
I've been experimenting with a few practical ways to create this separation, and I'll be honest'it's a work in progress. Here's what I'm doing right now:

- **Building Tools for Separation**: One of the moves I'm excited about is creating an AI-powered blog editor using Grok and Google Apps Script (you can read the nitty-gritty here: [Building an AI-Powered Blog Editor](https://garyteh.com/2025-12-02-building-an-ai-powered-blog-editor-with-grok-and-google-apps-script.html)). This tool helps me streamline my writing and reflections, making it easier to keep personal content separate from project updates. It's a small but meaningful step'tech helping me draw boundaries.
- **Redirecting Conversations**: When team members or folks in the community come to me for advice or input, I'm starting to point them to relevant blog posts instead of diving into long, one-off discussions that tie me deeper into the project's day-to-day. It's not about being distant'it's about building resources that outlive my direct involvement and let others take ownership.

Here's what that looks like in practice: Instead of being the go-to person for every idea or question, I'm creating a space where my thoughts live independently, and the project can start to speak for itself through the team and systems we're building. It's liberating, even if it feels a bit weird at first.

#### Section 3: The Emotional Side of Letting Go
I'd be lying if I said this decoupling doesn't come with some mixed feelings. There's a part of me that *loves* being the face of the project'it's validating, you know' But there's another part that knows clinging to that role isn't sustainable. I've had moments where I worry'will the project lose its soul if I step back' Will I lose a piece of myself if I'm not as hands-on' These are real questions, and I don't have all the answers yet.
